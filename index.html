The Occoquan River

The name Occoquan is derived from an Algonquian word commonly interpreted as “at the end of the water,” referring to the river’s fall line where navigable waters meet the rapids. This location has long served as a natural point of gathering for travel, trade, and community.

Over centuries, the Occoquan River has connected Indigenous communities, colonial industry, Revolutionary-era supply networks, and today’s maritime life—reflecting the enduring role of waterways in shaping the region and the nation.

About the Occoquan River Maritime Association (ORMA)

The Occoquan River Maritime Association (ORMA) is a community-driven organization rooted in a shared connection to the Occoquan River and the people who live, work, and recreate along its waters.

ORMA brings together boaters, marinas, yacht clubs, public safety partners, and local residents to encourage cooperation, stewardship, and respect for the river. Its work centers on safe boating practices, care for the water, and strengthening the community that depends on it.

Through gatherings such as the Blessing of the Fleet, ORMA creates space for reflection, remembrance, and connection—recognizing those who have lived and labored on the river while affirming a shared commitment to safety and mutual respect on the water today.

In the context of America’s 250th Anniversary, ORMA’s work reflects the importance of community in shaping the nation’s story, and the role that rivers like the Occoquan continue to play in bringing people together.
