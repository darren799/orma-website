The Occoquan River & the Birth of a Nation

The Occoquan River has been a vital waterway for thousands of years. Indigenous communities relied on it for travel, food, and trade. By the 18th century, it became one of Virginia’s most productive inland ports.

During the American Revolution, flour milled along the Occoquan supplied General George Washington’s Continental Army. The river’s protected location helped keep these supplies safe from British naval forces on the Potomac.
