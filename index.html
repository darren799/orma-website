The Occoquan River & America’s Founding

The Occoquan River was a critical inland waterway during the colonial period. Its mills produced flour that supplied the Continental Army, while its protected location helped safeguard vital resources during the Revolutionary War.

Pre-1700s: Indigenous communities rely on the river
1720s–1770s: Major colonial port and milling center
1775–1783: Support for Washington’s army

Student & Educator Resources

1. Lesson Overview: Rivers & the American Revolution

Students explore how rivers like the Occoquan supported colonial trade, transportation, and military logistics. Emphasis is placed on how geography influenced the success of the American Revolution.

2. Student Worksheet: Build a Historical Timeline

Students create a timeline tracing the Occoquan River from Indigenous use through the Revolutionary War to modern-day commemorations. Encourages chronological thinking and cause-and-effect analysis.

3. Virtual Field Trip Guide (Classroom or Home)

A guided discussion experience that walks students through the Occoquan River’s geography, colonial mills, and maritime traditions. Designed for use with maps, photos, or recorded video.

4. Discussion Prompts & Primary-Source Thinking

  • Why were rivers essential to colonial economies?
  • How did local communities support the Continental Army?
  • What risks did civilians take during the Revolutionary War?
  • How do waterways still shape communities today?

5. Virginia Standards of Learning (SOL) Alignment

  • Grade 4: Virginia Studies – Geography & colonial economy
  • Grade 5: U.S. History – Causes and outcomes of the Revolution
  • Middle School: Civics, regional history, critical thinking
  • High School: U.S. History & Government – primary sources
