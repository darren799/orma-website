The Occoquan River & the Town of Occoquan

The Occoquan River has been a vital waterway for thousands of years. Indigenous peoples relied on the river for transportation, fishing, and trade long before European settlement. Its location at the fall line—where freshwater meets the tidal Potomac system—made it uniquely suited for commerce and industry.

By the early 18th century, the river had become one of colonial Virginia’s most productive inland ports. Mills, warehouses, and shipyards lined its banks, and the Town of Occoquan emerged as a bustling center for flour production and export. Goods produced here were shipped throughout the American colonies and the Caribbean.

During the American Revolution, Occoquan played a critical but often overlooked role. Flour milled along the Occoquan River was supplied to General George Washington’s Continental Army. Because the river lay inland from British naval patrols on the Potomac, it provided a relatively secure supply route during wartime.

The Town of Occoquan was formally incorporated in 1804, but its identity as a working waterfront had already been firmly established. Today, Occoquan remains one of the oldest preserved river towns in Northern Virginia.

2026 Blessing of the Fleet

The ORMA Blessing of the Fleet is a time-honored maritime tradition offering prayers for safety, protection, and prosperity for all who work or recreate on the water.

In 2026, the ceremony holds special meaning as part of America’s Semiquincentennial, symbolically linking modern mariners with the generations who supported the birth of the United States.

  • Date: Saturday, May 16, 2026
  • Location: Occoquan River, Occoquan, Virginia
  • Open To: Recreational and commercial vessels, families, and the public
