Indigenous History

The Occoquan River takes its name from an Algonquian term commonly interpreted as “at the end of the water.” Indigenous peoples, including the Doeg and related Algonquian-speaking communities, lived along the river and relied on it for transportation, fishing, and trade.

Colonial and Revolutionary Era

During the colonial period, the river became an important industrial and transportation corridor. Mills, warehouses, and ports developed near the fall line, supplying flour and goods to the Continental Army during the Revolutionary War.

African American History

Enslaved and later free African Americans played a vital role in the river’s economic life, working in mills, on boats, and in riverfront communities. Their contributions are an essential part of the Occoquan’s story.

Maritime Community Today

Today, the Occoquan River is home to Prince William Marina, Occoquan Harbor Marina, Belmont Bay Harbor Marina, Holly Acres on the Water, and multiple yacht clubs and maritime organizations that continue the river’s working and recreational traditions.

The Blessing of the Fleet

Historically, blessings of the fleet emerged as a way to seek protection for those who make their livelihoods on the water. In 2026, the ORMA Blessing of the Fleet honors that tradition while commemorating America’s 250th Anniversary.
