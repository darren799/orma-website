The Occoquan River & the American Revolution

During the 18th century, the Occoquan River served as a strategic inland port. Flour milled along its banks supplied the Continental Army, supporting the fight for independence and demonstrating the importance of local waterways to national success.

Student & Educator Resources

1. Complete Lesson Plan

Objective: Students will analyze how rivers supported colonial economies and military logistics during the American Revolution.

Background: Rivers were the safest and most efficient transportation routes in the 1700s. The Occoquan River connected mills, farms, and ports to the Continental Army.

Procedure:

  • Introduce river geography
  • Examine a colonial map
  • Discuss supply routes
  • Connect local history to national independence

Assessment: Short written response or group discussion.

2. Printable Student Worksheet

  1. Why were rivers more reliable than roads in the 1700s?
  2. What goods were produced along the Occoquan River?
  3. How did civilians support the Revolutionary War?
  4. Why is the Occoquan River still important today?

3. Virtual Field Trip (Scripted)

Imagine standing on the Occoquan River in 1776. You see mills grinding grain, boats loading flour, and merchants preparing shipments for Washington’s army. This river connected local labor to national independence.

Teacher Prompt: Ask students how geography shaped daily life.

4. Primary-Source Discussion Questions

  • Elementary: Why did people live near rivers?
  • Middle School: How did rivers affect wartime strategy?
  • High School: How did civilian labor contribute to independence?
