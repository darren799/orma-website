The Occoquan River & America’s Founding

The Occoquan River was a strategic inland waterway during the colonial era. Flour milled along its banks supplied the Continental Army, while its protected location helped safeguard critical supply routes during the American Revolution.

Pre-1700s: Indigenous communities rely on the river
1720s–1770s: Major colonial milling and trade center
1775–1783: Support for Washington’s army

Student & Educator Resources

1. Complete Lesson Plan

Objective: Analyze how rivers supported colonial economies and military logistics.

Background: Rivers were the safest transportation routes of the 18th century. The Occoquan River linked mills, farms, and ports to the Continental Army.

Procedure:

  • Introduce colonial river geography
  • Examine Revolutionary-era supply routes
  • Discuss civilian contributions to independence

Assessment: Short written response or class discussion.

2. Printable Student Worksheet

  1. Why were rivers more reliable than roads in the 1700s?
  2. What goods were produced along the Occoquan River?
  3. How did civilians support the Revolutionary War?
  4. Why does the Occoquan River remain important today?

3. Virtual Field Trip (Guided Script)

Imagine standing along the Occoquan River in 1776. You see mills grinding grain, boats loading flour, and merchants preparing shipments for Washington’s army. This river connected local labor to national independence.

4. Primary-Source Discussion Questions

  • Elementary: Why did people build towns near rivers?
  • Middle School: How did rivers influence wartime strategy?
  • High School: How did civilian labor contribute to independence?
