The History of the Occoquan River

The Occoquan River has served as a vital waterway in Northern Virginia for centuries. From its early use by Indigenous peoples, to its role in colonial trade, milling, and shipbuilding, the river has long been a lifeline for the surrounding communities.

During the 18th and 19th centuries, the Town of Occoquan flourished as a commercial port, exporting grain, lumber, and goods throughout the region. Today, the river remains a treasured natural and cultural resource, supporting recreation, wildlife, and maritime traditions.

The Blessing of the Fleet

The Blessing of the Fleet is a time-honored maritime tradition dating back hundreds of years. The ceremony offers prayers for safety, prosperity, and calm seas for vessels and those who sail them.

On the Occoquan River, this event brings together boaters, families, clergy, and the community to celebrate our shared maritime heritage and to honor those who make their lives on the water.
