The Occoquan River: A Waterway at the Birth of a Nation

The Occoquan River has been a vital waterway for thousands of years. Indigenous peoples first relied on it for transportation, food, and trade. During the colonial era, the river became one of Virginia’s most productive inland ports.

In the years leading up to the American Revolution, flour milled along the Occoquan River helped supply the Continental Army under General George Washington. The river’s location provided a strategic advantage by supporting logistics while remaining protected from British naval forces.

Pre-1700s: Indigenous use of the river
1720s–1770s: Major colonial milling port
1775–1783: Support for the Revolutionary War

For Educators
