The Occoquan River & Town of Occoquan

The Occoquan River has been a lifeline of commerce, culture, and community for thousands of years. Long before European settlement, Indigenous peoples used the river for transportation, fishing, and trade, recognizing its unique location where freshwater meets the tidal Potomac system.

By the early 18th century, the river had become one of colonial Virginia’s most productive inland ports. The Town of Occoquan developed as a bustling center for milling, shipbuilding, and trade. Its mills produced large quantities of flour, which were exported throughout the American colonies and the Caribbean.

During the American Revolution, Occoquan’s strategic importance grew even greater. Flour milled along the Occoquan River was supplied to General George Washington’s Continental Army, helping sustain troops during critical campaigns. The river’s inland location offered protection from British naval forces operating along the Potomac River.

The Town of Occoquan was officially incorporated in 1804, but its identity as a working waterfront was firmly established decades earlier. Today, Occoquan remains one of the oldest preserved river towns in Northern Virginia, retaining its historic character and strong connection to the water.

2026 Blessing of the Fleet

The ORMA Blessing of the Fleet is a traditional maritime ceremony offering prayers for safety, protection, and prosperity for all who navigate the waters of the Occoquan River and beyond. Rooted in centuries-old seafaring customs, the blessing honors the enduring relationship between faith, community, and the maritime world.

In 2026, this ceremony carries added significance as part of America’s Semiquincentennial. By blessing today’s vessels, ORMA symbolically connects modern mariners with the generations of river workers, millers, and sailors who supported the birth of the United States.
